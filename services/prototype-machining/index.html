Prototype Machining · No Minimum · Fast Quote

Prototype Machining in Texas

Prototype machining for engineers with a design file and a deadline — single-part and low-volume prototype machining runs, no minimum order, real DFM feedback, and quotes back in 24–48 hours. Exotic and non-standard materials welcome.

Start a Job → (936) 291-7827

Most prototype machining requests start the same way: an engineer has a design file, a deadline, and no interest in a minimum-order quantity or a two-week quote turnaround. That's exactly what this service is built around. We quote single-piece and low-volume runs the same way we quote production work — real pricing, real lead time, no padding to hit an arbitrary minimum.

Prototype work is also where design-for-manufacturability (DFM) feedback earns its keep. If a pocket depth, a corner radius, or a called-out tolerance is going to blow up your cost or cycle time, we flag it at quote time — before you're paying for a second iteration because the first one came back wrong. And because we run oilfield-grade exotic alloys (Inconel 718, titanium, 17-4 PH) as weekly work rather than special-order material, a prototype doesn't have to be limited to 6061 aluminum just because it's early in the design cycle. If the final part needs to be Inconel, prototype it in Inconel.

We route prototype work across whichever platform actually fits the part rather than forcing everything onto one machine. A single turned shaft with a quick-turn feature might go straight to a manual lathe with no CAM programming at all. A complex prismatic bracket goes to the Fadal or YCM for CNC milling. A design that's likely to iterate two or three times before it's locked stays on a platform where we can adjust between revisions without re-programming from scratch. That flexibility is the actual product here — not just fast turnaround, but the judgment to put your part on the right machine the first time.

What "prototype machining" means at B&R

Prototype machining at B&R covers single-part and low-volume runs (2 to roughly 50 pieces) where the point is proving out a design, not filling a production schedule. There's no minimum order quantity — you pay for setup and the part itself, not a padded quantity dressed up to look like a real production run. That distinction matters because a lot of shops quietly treat "prototype" work as a loss-leader they rush through; we quote it the same disciplined way we quote a 500-piece production job, with real pricing and a real lead time attached to it.

Turnaround: file to shipped part

Upload a STEP file (preferred), IGES, native CAD, or a dimensioned PDF drawing along with your material spec and quantity. Prototype machining quotes come back in 24–48 hours, often same-day for existing customers with a straightforward part. Once the quote is approved, stocked-material prototype machining jobs with straightforward geometry ship in 3–7 days; complex geometry, tight tolerances, or alloy that has to come from the mill runs 1–3 weeks. Every prototype gets the same critical-dimension inspection as a production part before it leaves the shop.

DFM feedback on your file

Design-for-manufacturability review is built into every prototype machining quote, not sold as an add-on. If a pocket depth, an internal corner radius that doesn't match standard tooling, or a called-out tolerance is going to blow up your cost or cycle time, we flag it at quote time — before you're paying for a second prototype machining iteration because the first one came back wrong. That feedback loop is often the actual value of a prototype machining run: catching a manufacturability problem on paper instead of on the shop floor.

Materials we prototype in

We run Inconel 718, titanium Ti-6Al-4V, 17-4 PH, and other oilfield- and aerospace-grade alloys as weekly work, so if your final part needs to be Inconel, we can prototype machine it in Inconel from the first article. From prototype to production

Prototype machining and production machining run on the same shop floor with the same tolerance discipline — the difference is quantity and process lock-in. Once a design is proven through prototype machining, moving to a 100-to-10,000-piece production run means the same alloy, the same tooling strategy, and a locked first-article process rather than starting over with a new supplier. See our CNC production machining service page for how we structure that step once your prototype machining phase is done.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a minimum order for prototype machining?

No. We regularly run single-piece prototype machining orders. You pay for the setup and the part, not a padded minimum quantity — that's the whole point of a prototype machining run.

How fast can I get a prototype machining quote?

24-48 hours is standard for a prototype machining quote once we have a STEP file (or drawing) and material spec. Existing customers with a straightforward part often hear back same-day.

Do you give design-for-manufacturability (DFM) feedback on prototype machining jobs?

Yes — it's part of how we quote every prototype machining job. If a feature is going to blow up your cost or cycle time (a deep thin-wall pocket, an internal corner radius that doesn't match standard tooling, a tolerance tighter than the part needs), we'll flag it before you commit to a price, not after the first article comes back wrong.

What file formats do you accept for a prototype machining quote?

STEP (.stp/.step) is preferred — it's the cleanest for quoting and programming. We also work from IGES, native SolidWorks files, and PDF drawings with GD&T. Send what you have; we'll tell you if we need more.

Can you do prototype machining in exotic or non-standard materials?

Yes — Inconel 718, titanium, 17-4 PH, and other oilfield/aerospace alloys are weekly work here, not special-order experiments. If your design calls for a material a general job shop won't touch, that's exactly the kind of prototype machining we're built for.

What's a realistic prototype machining turnaround from quote to shipped part?

For stocked material with a straightforward geometry: 3-7 days after quote approval. Complex geometry, tight tolerances, or material that has to come from the mill: 1-3 weeks. We'll give you a real number at quote time, not a placeholder.

Do you charge more for prototype machining than production parts?

Per-piece cost is typically higher on a single prototype machining run than it would be at production volume, because setup cost amortizes over fewer parts — that's normal across the industry, not a prototype markup. We price it straight and explain the breakdown if you ask.
