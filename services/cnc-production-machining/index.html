What we call "production" here

A production job at B&R starts around 100 pieces and runs into the thousands — not a single prototype, and not a padded quote dressed up to look like volume. The difference from prototype work isn't just quantity: it's the commitment behind it. Production customers get a proven first-article before the full run cuts, a locked process (same fixture, same tool life targets, same inspection plan) for the life of the program, and delivery that matches how you actually consume the part — all 2,000 pieces at once, or released in phases against a blanket order as your schedule calls them off. If you need one part to prove a design, that's prototype work and we'll say so. If you need the same part 40 times a year for the next three years, that's what this page is built for.

The machines that make production run economics work

Production pricing only works when the machine, not the operator, is carrying the repetition. Our Hwacheon lathes run bar feeders for unattended overnight production on turned components — the machine keeps cutting after the shift ends, which is the single biggest lever on cost-per-part for a multi-hundred-piece run. The Samsung SL sub-spindle lathes complete a part in a single operation instead of two separate setups, cutting both cycle time and the chance of a second-operation error creeping into a long run. For long-bore production work — downhole tool bodies, hydraulic cylinder bores, anything needing straightness over length — the UNISIG-class deep-hole drilling machine holds sub-thousandth-per-foot straightness at production pace, work that would eat a standard lathe alive over a 500-piece run. None of this is exotic capability for a one-off; it's the infrastructure that makes the 400th part cost close to what the 40th part cost.

Materials we run at production volume

Inconel 718 is weekly production work here, not a special-order alloy — aged and solution-annealed, dialed feeds and speeds, and tooling budgeted into the quote instead of discovered mid-run. Super Duplex 2507 holds its chloride-resistant phase balance across a full production lot because we control cutting temperature the same way on part 1 and part 1,000. 17-4 PH in aerospace-fastener-relevant conditions (H900 through H1150) is common production material. 4140/4340 pre-hard covers the bulk of oilfield structural production runs. Documentation buyers on production programs expect

Every production job carries material traceability by heat and lot, a first-article inspection report before the run proceeds, and in-process gauging through the length of the run — not just a spot-check at the end. Mill Test Reports and a certificate of conformance ship with delivery. For buyers running formal quality plans, we support customer-specific Inspection and Test Plans (ITPs) and blanket-order release tracking against your PO schedule. Straight talk on the boundary: we run a disciplined, documented process, but B&R doesn't hold a registered PPAP or AS9100 quality system. If your program requires a full PPAP submission package or AS9100 flow-down, say so at RFQ and we'll tell you plainly whether that's work for us or work for a registered supplier.

How production runs work at B&R

Quote against your print, material spec, and quantity — including the release structure if you need phased delivery rather than one shipment. First article gets cut, measured, and approved before the balance of the run proceeds, so nobody discovers a problem at part 300. In-process gauging holds the process on-spec through the run. Final CMM verification confirms critical dimensions before anything ships. For blanket orders, we track releases against your schedule so parts show up when your line needs them, not just when the shop finishes cutting.

Frequently asked questions

What quantity is realistic per month for Inconel 718?

Depends on part complexity and setup, but multi-hundred-piece monthly volumes on turned Inconel 718 components are realistic once the process is proven on first article. Tell us the part and target monthly quantity at RFQ and we'll give you a straight capacity number, not a guess.

Do you support blanket orders and releases?

Yes — this is standard structure for our production customers. We quote and tool the full blanket quantity, then release parts against your PO schedule instead of shipping everything at once. Tell us your release cadence at quote time.

How do you handle material lot control on multi-run programs?

Every heat and lot is tracked from raw material through finished part, documented on the MTR and certificate of conformance for each shipment. On multi-run programs, we flag it if a new material lot enters the program so your quality records stay clean.

What's your setup vs. run cost ratio?

Setup cost is fixed per job; run cost drops per piece as quantity increases, which is the entire economic case for production quantities over one-off prototype pricing. We break the two out separately in the quote so you can see exactly what volume buys you.

Can you commit to fixed pricing on repeat programs?

Yes, within the material-cost assumptions we quote against — if raw material pricing moves significantly during a long program, we say so before it affects your PO, not after. Ask about locking pricing for a defined program term.

What's your production-part tolerance capability versus prototype?

The same ±0.0005" routine tolerance applies at production volume as on a single prototype — the difference is that a proven, locked process holds that tolerance consistently across the full run, verified with in-process gauging rather than one-off inspection.

Do you support customer-owned tooling and fixtures?

Yes. If your program already has qualified fixtures or gauging from a prior supplier, we can run against customer-owned tooling. We'll review it against our machines during quoting to confirm fit.
