What we machine on frac pump fluid ends

A complete frac pump fluid end starts as a stress-relieved forging and becomes a precision pressure vessel — cross-drilled suction and discharge bores, valve seat pockets, packing bores, and a discharge cross body, all machined to hold pressure cycle after cycle without fatigue cracking at the bore intersections. We rough machine fluid end blocks from raw forgings and finish machine to final tolerance, and we run wear rings and sleeves as standalone rebuild-kit components when a fleet just needs the wear parts, not a new block.

Fluid end platforms we've machined for

Frac pump fluid end machining work at B&R spans the platforms operating across Texas frac fleets — Gardner Denver / Continental Emsco / GD Energy Products quintuplex and triplex designs, Weir SPM, NOV / National Oilwell Varco, Serva, and Halliburton pump lines. We machine to the OEM print when we have it and reverse-engineer from a sample or a worn block when we don't. If your fleet is running a legacy platform the OEM no longer supports, that's routine sustainment work for us, not a special project.

Alloys for frac pump fluid end service

4340 pre-hard covers the bulk of standard-service fluid end blocks — a strength-to-machinability balance that suits a part getting cross-drilled and bored repeatedly. 17-4 PH steps up for higher-pressure or higher-cycle-life service where the extra strength and corrosion resistance justify the material cost. Inconel-clad and full Inconel 718 fluid ends go where the frac fluid chemistry is chloride- or H2S-critical — sour service that would fatigue-crack a standard 4340 or 17-4 block long before its design life. We'll talk through the trade-off with your engineering team at RFQ rather than defaulting to whatever alloy is easiest for us to run.

Rebuild kit and aftermarket fluid end work

Not every fluid end job is a new block. Plungers, valve seats, valve bodies, packing rings, and wear sleeves are the parts that wear out fastest in service, and we run them at aftermarket volume in the same alloys as new-manufacture work — 17-4 PH plungers, Nitronic seats, Inconel-clad wear components where sour service demands it. Fleets that standardize on a rebuild-kit supplier instead of sourcing piecemeal get faster turnaround and consistent material quality stage after stage.

Emergency response + capacity

A cracked fluid end mid-stage is lost frac-fleet day-rate, and same-day to next-day turnaround is realistic for existing customers when the material is on the shelf — call (936) 291-7827 and say so. During peak frac season we accommodate blanket-order OEM programs and rolling-forecast capacity so a fleet's rebuild-kit supply doesn't run out mid-quarter. NDAs are standard practice for OEM-restricted prints, signed on request before a proprietary drawing crosses our desk.

Blanket orders with scheduled releases are the normal structure for repeat frac pump fluid end programs, the same way they work across our other contract-manufacturing customers.

Frequently asked questions

Do you machine complete frac pump fluid end blocks from raw forgings?

Yes — rough machining from stress-relieved forgings through finish machining, cross-drilling, threading, and inspection to your print or ours.

What's your capacity to rough machine quintuplex fluid ends?

Depends on quantity and cadence, but multi-unit monthly capacity on quintuplex fluid end blocks is realistic once the process is proven on first article. Tell us the part and target volume at RFQ and we'll give a straight capacity number.

Which frac pump platforms have you machined fluid ends for?

Gardner Denver / Continental Emsco / GD Energy Products, Weir SPM, NOV, Serva, and Halliburton pump lines — quintuplex and triplex designs both. If your fluid end print or sample is from a platform not listed, send it and we'll tell you honestly whether it's a fit.

Do you sign NDAs for OEM fluid end programs?

Yes, routinely, before a proprietary fluid end print or process detail crosses our desk.

What alloys do you recommend for high-cycle fluid end service?

17-4 PH for most higher-pressure, higher-cycle-life duty. Inconel-clad or full Inconel 718 when the frac fluid chemistry is chloride- or H2S-critical. 4340 pre-hard remains the right call for standard-service duty where the extra alloy cost doesn't buy meaningful life.

Can you handle blanket-order fluid end production?

Yes — this is the standard structure for our repeat frac pump fluid end and OEM rebuild-kit customers. We tool the full blanket quantity and release parts against your PO schedule.

Do you machine aftermarket rebuild kit parts (plungers, valve seats)?

Yes — plungers, valve seats, valve bodies, packing rings, and wear sleeves at aftermarket volume, in the same alloys as new-manufacture fluid end work.

How fast can you turn around emergency fluid end work?

Same-day to next-day for existing customers when the material — 4340, 17-4 PH, or Inconel 718 — is on the shelf. Call (936) 291-7827 and say it's a fluid end down.
