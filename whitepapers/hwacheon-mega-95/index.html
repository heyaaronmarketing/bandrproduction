Hwacheon Mega 95 — Rattle & Hum

Long-bed CNC lathe for extended shafts, rollers, rods, and structural round parts. 16" OD × 120" L max turning.

Rattle & Hum · long-bed heavy-duty CNC lathe

Ten feet of turning length is a specialty capability. Long drive shafts, extended mandrels, rig-floor rollers, oil-and-gas structural rods — the parts that don't fit on a 4- or 6-foot lathe. Rattle & Hum lets us keep this class of work in-house instead of outsourcing the finish turning after roughing on a larger machine somewhere else.

What Rattle & Hum is for

Rattle & Hum is the length machine: 16" OD × 120" L on a gap bed, with a spindle range that drops to 40 RPM. Ten feet of turning capacity is not a marginal upgrade over a 42-inch lathe — it is a different category of part. Extended shafts, rollers, rods, long structural rounds, and multi-diameter shafting that would otherwise have to be made in sections and joined all become single-piece parts here, which removes a joint, a tolerance stack, and a failure mode.

Pick this machine when

  • The part is long — shafts, rollers, rods, screw stock, long structural rounds up to 120".
  • You've been quoting the part in sections because nobody local can turn it in one piece.
  • Low-RPM heavy cutting on large-diameter stock, where 40 RPM at the bottom of the range is a working speed and not a formality.
  • The gap bed's extra swing is needed for a flanged or stepped feature near one end.

When it's the wrong machine

Anything needing a big bore through the spindle — that's Texaco Thunder's 10-inch through-hole, and it's the reason both machines exist. Short parts don't belong here either; they'll run faster and cheaper on a Hi-Eco 35 cell. And 16" OD is a real ceiling — parts above it go to Big Thicket Express at 22".

From the floor

At 120 inches, straightness is the specification that quietly governs everything. Long stock deflects under its own weight and under tool pressure, and thermal growth over a long cut is measurable rather than theoretical. Roughing and finishing get planned as separate thermal events with support staged along the length, which is why long shaft work takes longer to set up here and comes out straight.
