Precision Machining

Tolerance-obsessed precision machining for oilfield-critical and aerospace dimensional accuracy — CMM-verified, fully traceable, from our precision machining shop in New Waverly, TX.

Start a Job → (936) 291-7827

"Precision machining" gets used loosely across the industry — a lot of shops that quote ±0.005" call themselves precision machining shops. We hold tighter than that as a matter of routine, and we back it up with in-process gauging and CMM verification on every critical dimension, not just the ones a customer specifically calls out. On tight setups with fresh tooling, ±0.0001" is achievable on our lathes and mills; ±0.0005" is the number we quote as standard practice on critical dimensions across the fleet doing precision machining every week.

What makes precision machining repeatable at this level isn't the machine alone — it's the discipline around it. Every job that carries a tolerance callout gets a first-article inspection with CMM data before the rest of the run proceeds. Cutting parameters are dialed per alloy, not guessed: coated-carbide insert selection, feed rate, and coolant strategy change between 17-4 PH, Inconel 718, and Super Duplex 2507 because work-hardening and thermal behavior differ enough that a one-size-fits-all approach ruins tolerance on the second or third part in a run.

This matters most where dimensional accuracy is the whole point of the part. A downhole tool body with a sealing-surface finish that's off by a few tenths doesn't seal downhole. An aerospace bracket with a true-position error outside spec doesn't assemble. A valve stem with the wrong concentricity galls in service. Our precision machining work concentrates in oil & gas (where oilfield-critical dimensional accuracy on API-adjacent hardware is non-negotiable) and aerospace (where profile and true-position tolerances are called out on every drawing), but the same setup discipline applies to any precision machining part that lands on this list.

Frequently asked questions

What's the tightest tolerance you can hold?

±0.0001" is achievable on critical dimensions with a fresh setup and disciplined process control. ±0.0005" is what we quote as routine, standard practice across the fleet on critical features.

Do you CMM-inspect every part, or only on request?

Every job with a tolerance callout gets first-article CMM verification before the run proceeds, plus in-process gauging through the run. It's built into how we run precision work, not an optional add-on.

What alloys do you hold tight tolerances on?

Inconel 718, Super Duplex 2507, 17-4 PH, and Titanium Ti-6Al-4V are the four we run weekly at precision tolerances. Each has its own dialed-in feeds, speeds, and tooling to prevent work-hardening or thermal drift from eating your tolerance band.

What documentation comes with precision parts?

Full material traceability by heat and lot, first-article inspection reports with CMM data, Mill Test Reports, and certificates of conformance. Customer-specific inspection and test plans (ITPs) available on request.

Why does oilfield-critical dimensional accuracy matter so much?

Downhole and wellhead parts seal, mate, and actuate under pressure and temperature they can't be adjusted for in the field. A sealing surface, a bore concentricity, or a thread tolerance that's off doesn't fail gracefully — it fails downhole or at the wellhead. We machine to the tolerance the application actually needs, every time.

Do you offer precision machining for prototypes, or only production?

Both. Prototype-scale precision machining often runs through manual or low-volume CNC setups where accuracy still matters even at quantity one — see our prototype machining service for that specific workflow.

Is precision machining more expensive than standard CNC work?

Precision machining costs more per part because of the added CMM verification, first-article inspection, and slower, more disciplined feeds — but we don't apply that overhead to parts that don't need it. Tell us your actual tolerance requirement and we'll quote precision machining only where the drawing calls for it.

What's your lead time on tight-tolerance parts?

1–3 weeks is typical for precision parts with first-article inspection, depending on alloy availability and complexity. Emergency turnarounds are available for existing customers on stocked material.
