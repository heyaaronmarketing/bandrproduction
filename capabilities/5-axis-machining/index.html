5 Axis Machining Services

5 axis machining, handled honestly — compound-angle and multi-face CNC machining with 4th-axis rotary indexing in-house on our Fadal and YCM VMCs, plus true simultaneous 5 axis machining managed through a vetted partner network. Texas-based project management, one point of contact.

"5 axis machining" covers a wide range of real capability, and buyers searching for it usually mean one of two different things: parts that need to be machined from several angles without being re-fixtured (often called 3+2, or positional 5-axis), or parts that need the cutting tool to move through five axes simultaneously to cut a continuous contoured surface (true simultaneous 5 axis machining — think impellers, turbine blades, complex aerospace ductwork). The two require very different machine architecture, and it matters which one your part actually needs.

Here's where B&R stands today, straight up: our vertical machining centers — the Fadal VMC 4020 and YCM TV-188 B — run rotary 4th-axis indexing, which lets us machine multiple faces of a part in a single setup without a full simultaneous 5-axis head. That covers a real chunk of what people mean when they ask for "5 axis machining": compound-angle brackets, multi-face bodies, and parts where the win is eliminating re-fixture error, not cutting a continuously curved surface. We do not currently offer true simultaneous 5 axis machining on our own floor, and we're not going to tell you otherwise to win a quote.

For work that genuinely needs simultaneous 5 axis machining, we manage it through a small network of vetted partner shops we've worked with directly — we handle the quoting, the material sourcing, the quality documentation, and the single point of contact, and the partner shop runs the simultaneous 5 axis machining under our oversight. That's a legitimate model a lot of buyers are comfortable with: you get one relationship and one accountable party, and the part gets made on the right machine instead of forced onto the wrong one. If you're not sure whether your part needs 3+2 or true simultaneous 5 axis machining, send the print — we'll tell you honestly which one it is and quote it accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Does B&R offer 5 axis machining in-house?

Not true simultaneous 5 axis machining — no, and we won't tell you otherwise. Our Fadal VMC 4020 and YCM TV-188 B vertical machining centers run rotary 4th-axis indexing for multi-face (3+2) setups. For simultaneous 5 axis machining, we manage the work through vetted partner shops.

What's the difference between 3+2 and true simultaneous 5 axis machining?

3+2 (positional 5-axis) means the part is indexed to a compound angle, then machined with standard 3-axis motion — great for multi-face parts and compound-angle brackets. True simultaneous 5 axis machining moves the tool through all five axes at once to cut continuously contoured surfaces like impellers or turbine geometry. Most parts described as "needing 5 axis machining" actually need 3+2, which we run in-house.

Can you quote true simultaneous 5 axis machining even though you don't own the machine?

Yes. We quote it, manage material and quality documentation, and run it through a vetted partner shop under our oversight. You get one point of contact and one accountable party — we just don't pretend the work happens on our floor when it doesn't.

What tolerances do you hold on 3+2/4th-axis milled parts?

±0.0005" on critical dimensions is routine on our VMC platform, with first-article CMM verification. The same discipline applies whether the setup is 3-axis, 3+2, or routed through a partner for simultaneous 5 axis machining.

What materials do you run for 5 axis machining-style work?

Titanium Ti-6Al-4V, Inconel 718, 17-4 PH, and 6061/7075 aluminum are the most common on compound-angle and multi-face parts. Full material traceability regardless of which platform runs the job.

How do I know if my part needs 3+2 or true 5 axis machining?

Send the print. If the part has flat or simple-curved faces at different angles, 3+2 almost always works and stays in-house. If it has continuously blended contoured surfaces, it likely needs true simultaneous 5 axis machining and goes through our partner network. We'll tell you which before you commit to a quote.
