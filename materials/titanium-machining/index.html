Frequently asked questions

Do you handle DFARS-compliant titanium?

We machine it, but we don't source it as DFARS-certified — B&R doesn't run a DFARS specialty-metals compliance program. If your program flows DFARS down, supply the Ti-6Al-4V through a compliant channel and we'll machine it with full MTR and heat/lot traceability. For non-DFARS titanium work we source and machine start to finish.

What titanium grades do you machine?

Ti-6Al-4V (Grade 5) is our primary titanium. Other grades (2, 4, 9, 23 ELI) available on request depending on program requirements and material availability.

What's the typical lead time on titanium work?

2–5 weeks for standard aerospace parts in stocked Ti-6Al-4V. Longer if the specific plate/bar spec needs to come from a specific mill run.

What makes titanium machining different from stainless or aluminum?

Titanium machining concentrates cutting heat at the tool tip because titanium has poor thermal conductivity — the heat can't escape into the chip or workpiece. Combined with titanium's tendency to gall and spring back, that means dull tools fail catastrophically fast. B&R's titanium machining practice: sharp coated inserts changed on schedule (not on wear), aggressive flood coolant, positive-rake geometry, controlled DOC to keep chip load in the sweet spot, and first-article CMM to catch any springback drift before it accumulates.

Which industries do you serve for titanium machining?

Titanium machining at B&R primarily serves aerospace (airframe brackets, engine mount hardware, structural fittings), defense (UAV components, weapon subsystem parts, lightweight structural), and specialty industrial (chemical process hardware, marine components). Medical-adjacent titanium machining is available for non-implant applications — implantable-grade certification is a separate program we don't currently hold.
