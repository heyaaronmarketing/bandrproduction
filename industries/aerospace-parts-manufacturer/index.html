The Parts Get Made Here. The Cert Lives With the Prime.

Plenty of aerospace parts manufacturing never touches a registered QMS requirement — R&D hardware, general aviation, private-aircraft rebuild, commercial-MRO components, and Tier-2 work performed under a certified prime's oversight. B&R has machined tight-tolerance aerospace hardware in Texas since 1994 and fits that lane deliberately. We are not AS9100 or Nadcap certified, and we say so before you spend an RFQ cycle finding out.

Where B&R fits — and where it doesn't

Say the honest part first: B&R Productions is not AS9100-certified and not Nadcap-accredited. If your program requires either flow-down — most primes' Tier-1 structural and flight-critical work does — you need a registered supplier, and we're not it. Where we do fit: aerospace programs and part categories that don't carry that requirement. A meaningful share of aerospace parts manufacturing lives there — R&D and prototype hardware before a program locks its supply chain, commercial aviation MRO components, general-aviation and private-aircraft rebuild parts, and Tier-2 subcontractor work performed under a certified prime that holds the QMS registration and flows documentation requirements down to us rather than requiring us to hold the certificate ourselves. What we are not positioning for: critical flight-control or primary structural hardware that must carry a Nadcap process certification — that work needs a Nadcap shop, full stop.

Aerospace components we routinely machine

Structural and mounting brackets, fasteners and fastener-adjacent hardware, actuator components, landing-gear parts (small-to-mid volume, not primary structural gear), and structural sub-parts that feed into a larger assembly. Alloys: 17-4 PH (the workhorse for structural brackets and hardware), Ti-6Al-4V for weight-critical structural components, 15-5 PH where higher toughness is called for, 4340 for high-strength structural parts, and Inconel 718 for high-temperature engine-adjacent hardware. If a part is currently in prototype or low-rate initial production and hasn't locked its long-term supplier yet, that's frequently exactly the stage where a shop like ours is the right fit — before the program requires a registered Tier-1 supply chain.

Tolerance and finish capability aerospace parts require

±0.0005" is routine on critical dimensions; ±0.0002" is achievable on the right feature with the right fixture, not a number we print on every drawing regardless of geometry. Surface finish held to Ra ≤32 µin where the print calls for it. Every job gets a first-article layout before production proceeds, plus CMM verification of critical dimensions — including true-position, profile, and runout tolerances read straight off your GD&T callouts, not approximated.

Documentation package for aerospace subcontractor programs

Material Test Reports with heat and lot traceability on every piece of stock. First-article inspection reports with CMM data. A certificate of conformance on every shipment. Customer-specific inspection requirements accommodated where they fall within what an unregistered shop can reasonably provide. What we can't produce: a Nadcap special-process certification, an AS9100 quality-system audit trail, or DFARS-compliant specialty-metals sourcing documentation. Tell us your documentation requirement at RFQ and we'll tell you plainly, before you commit a PO, whether it's something we can meet.

Who we work with

Private and general aviation owners and shops needing structural or replacement hardware. Aerospace MRO shops sourcing components outside OEM channels. Aerospace Tier-1 and Tier-2 subcontractors who need a Tier-2 machining supplier under their own certified oversight. Engineering teams in R&D and prototype phases who need real aerospace-alloy parts — not aluminum stand-ins — before a program's supply chain locks down.

Frequently asked questions

Are you AS9100 certified?

No. B&R Productions is not AS9100-registered and not Nadcap-accredited, and we lead with that rather than let you find out mid-RFQ. It matters when your prime's contract flows down a registered-QMS requirement — Tier-1 structural and flight-critical work almost always does, and that work needs a certified shop. It doesn't matter for a large share of aerospace parts manufacturing: R&D and prototype hardware, general-aviation and private-aircraft parts, commercial MRO components, and Tier-2 work performed under a certified prime's own oversight. Ask which category your part falls into and we'll tell you straight whether we're the right fit.

Do you machine Ti-6Al-4V?

Yes — Ti-6Al-4V (Grade 5) is regular work here, along with Grade 2 and Grade 23 (ELI) on request. Structural brackets and weight-critical hardware in titanium are common aerospace-parts jobs for us.

What's your smallest and largest aerospace part envelope?

Small precision-turned fastener-adjacent hardware up through mid-sized structural brackets and landing-gear-adjacent components fit our lathes and VMCs comfortably. Send your envelope and material with the RFQ and we'll confirm fit rather than guess.

Can you do aerospace prototyping and then move to production?

Yes — this is a common path for us. A part often starts as a single prototype or low-rate initial-production run in the actual aerospace alloy (not an aluminum stand-in), then moves to a production quantity once the design is locked. See our /services/prototype-machining/ and /services/cnc-production-machining/ pages for how each phase works.

What documentation do you provide?

Material Test Reports with heat/lot traceability, first-article inspection reports with CMM data, and a certificate of conformance on every shipment. We do not provide Nadcap special-process certifications, AS9100 quality-system documentation, or DFARS-compliant specialty-metals sourcing records — tell us your requirement at RFQ and we'll say plainly whether we can meet it.

Do you carry customer material?

Yes — customer-supplied and customer-specified aerospace-grade material is common, with the same heat/lot traceability applied as material we source ourselves.

Do you provide first-article inspection reports?

Yes, standard on every aerospace job — dimensional layout against the print, with CMM verification of critical dimensions including true-position, profile, and runout callouts where the drawing specifies them.

Have a aerospace parts part? Send the print — we'll quote it.
