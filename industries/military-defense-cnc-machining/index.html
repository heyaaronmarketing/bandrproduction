Common questions buyers ask

Who does military and defense CNC machining in Texas?

B&R Productions in New Waverly, Texas machines defense-adjacent components — ground vehicle parts, tactical equipment, weapon subsystems (non-classified), aviation structural, legacy sustainment — in 17-4 PH, Inconel 718, Ti-6Al-4V, 4140/4340, Nitronic 50/60, and 7075-T651 aluminum since 1994. Not ITAR-registered, not a classified facility. Non-export-controlled defense work only. Phone (936) 291-7827.

Is B&R Productions ITAR-registered?

No. B&R Productions is not ITAR-registered with the U.S. State Department's DDTC and does not accept export-controlled prints. That's a deliberate answer, not a dodge — export-controlled work belongs at a registered facility. Non-controlled defense-adjacent work is regular business for B&R. Proprietary prints stay in-shop under NDA.

Does B&R Productions do defense sustainment and legacy replacement parts?

Yes — this is the kind of work a 30-year job shop is genuinely well-suited to. Reverse-engineered replacements from a worn sample, feature-add on existing hardware, and one-off parts for platforms whose OEM has discontinued the SKU. Send the sample and whatever documentation survives; B&R measures it, verifies the alloy, and produces replacements with full traceability.

What does the DFARS specialty metals clause require, and how does B&R fit?

The DFARS specialty metals clause (DFARS 252.225-7009) restricts DoD contractors and subcontractors from acquiring specialty metals — steel, nickel-base alloys, cobalt-base alloys, titanium and titanium alloys, and zirconium — melted or produced outside the U.S. or an approved qualifying country on covered contracts. B&R Productions is not a DFARS-registered specialty-metals sourcing shop, but can machine customer-supplied DFARS-compliant material with the MTR paperwork chain intact, produce parts to your print and tolerance, and deliver a first-article inspection report plus certificate of conformance on the machined part.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle ITAR-controlled work?

No. ITAR compliance requires registration with the U.S. State Department's DDTC, and B&R does not hold that registration — so we don't accept export-controlled prints. That's a deliberate answer, not a dodge: you'd rather hear it now than three weeks into a program. Non-controlled defense-adjacent work is work we're equipped for and want, and proprietary prints stay in-shop under NDA.

What defense work is B&R actually a good fit for?

Non-export-controlled defense-adjacent hardware: ground-vehicle and equipment components, structural and mounting hardware, tooling and fixtures, and legacy sustainment parts reverse-engineered from a worn sample when the OEM has discontinued the SKU. We don't publish a list of programs or primes — we're a 30-year job shop, not a defense prime, and overstating that helps nobody. Tell us what the part is and we'll tell you plainly whether it's work we should be quoting.

Do you handle sustainment work for legacy defense platforms?

Standard defense CNC work: 2–5 weeks depending on complexity, alloy, and documentation depth. Rush and emergency sustainment: as fast as 3–5 days when material is on the shelf.

What is military machining and how does it differ from commercial CNC work?

Military machining is precision CNC work for defense-adjacent hardware — ground vehicles, tactical equipment, weapon subsystems, aviation structural, legacy sustainment. The difference from commercial work is the documentation package: heat and lot traceability by MTR, first-article inspection reports, CMM verification on every critical dimension, and a certificate of conformance on delivery. Some programs also require CAGE codes, DFARS-compliant material sourcing, and controlled document handling under NDA — we discuss each requirement at quote time.

Do you take on Texas military machining contracts?

Yes. We serve Texas-based primes, subcontractors, and MRO facilities from our New Waverly, TX shop. Non-ITAR military machining is our regular work — we ship on tight timelines to Houston, DFW, San Antonio, and out-of-state primes needing sustainment or production runs on defense-adjacent hardware.

What does the DFARS specialty metals clause require, and how does B&R fit?

The DFARS specialty metals clause (DFARS 252.225-7009) restricts DoD contractors and their subcontractors from acquiring specialty metals — steel, nickel-base alloys, cobalt-base alloys, titanium and titanium alloys, and zirconium — that were melted or produced outside the United States or an approved qualifying country, on certain covered contracts. It applies down the supply chain: if a defense prime's contract carries the DFARS specialty metals clause, any subcontractor supplying specialty-metal parts inherits the sourcing requirement. B&R Productions is not a DFARS-registered specialty-metals sourcing shop, and we don't hold a domestic-melt certification program — so we don't take responsibility for sourcing DFARS-compliant material end-to-end. What we can do: machine customer-supplied DFARS-compliant material with full heat/lot MTR traceability preserved from your certified stock, produce parts to your print and tolerance, and deliver a first-article inspection report + certificate of conformance on the machined part. If your prime flows the DFARS specialty metals clause down to you, supply the compliant stock through your qualified channel and we'll machine it with the paperwork chain intact. If your program doesn't carry the clause — commercial, sustainment on legacy platforms, non-DoD defense-adjacent — we source material through our normal channels and it's a non-issue.

Do you know when the DFARS specialty metals clause applies to my program?

Not authoritatively — your program contracting officer or the prime's supply-chain compliance function is the definitive source for whether DFARS specialty metals flow-down applies to a specific procurement. Rough shape: it applies to many DoD acquisitions of end items that contain specialty metals, with exceptions for commercial off-the-shelf items, certain foreign military sales, and a domestic-non-availability-determination path. When you send the RFQ, tell us whether the clause applies. If it does, supply the material through your qualified channel and we machine it. If it doesn't, we quote normally.
